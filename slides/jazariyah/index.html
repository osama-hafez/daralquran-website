Al-Muqaddimah Al-Jazariyyah

A Comprehensive Masterclass on تَجْوِيد (Tajweed)

In-depth theoretical foundations and practical application based on the renowned poem by Imam Muhammad Ibn Al-Jazari Ash-Shafi'i.

الْمُقَدِّمَة (The Introduction)

الْمُؤَلِّف (The Author)

The poem begins with a humble introduction of the author, Imam Ibn Al-Jazari, seeking the forgiveness of Allah.

الْحَمْد وَالصَّلَاة (Praise & Salutations)

Acknowledging that all praise belongs to Allah, followed by sending peace upon Prophet Muhammad (ﷺ).

الصَّحَابَة (The Companions)

Extending the salutations to the Prophet's family, companions, and those who recite and love the Quran.

يَقُولُ رَاجِي عَفْوِ رَبٍّ سَامِعِ
The one hoping for the pardon of a Hearing Lord says
مُحَمَّدُ بْنُ الْجَزَرِيِّ الشَّافِعِي
Muhammad, the son of Al-Jazari, the Shafi'i.
الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ وَصَلَّى اللَّهُ
Praise be to Allah, and may Allah exalt the mention of
عَلَى نَبِيِّهِ وَمُصْطَفَاهُ
His Prophet and Chosen one.
مُحَمَّدٍ وَآلِهِ وَصَحْبِهِ
Muhammad, his family, his companions
وَمُقْرِئِ الْقُرْآنِ مَعْ مُحِبِّهِ
and the teacher of the Quran along with its lover.

الْوُجُوب (The Obligation of Knowledge) - Part 1

1st
Primary Duty

The Prerequisite to Recitation

Before embarking on the recitation of the Quran, Imam Al-Jazari states it is an absolute obligation to acquire foundational knowledge.

  • مَخَارِج (Articulation Points): Where letters originate.
  • صِفَات (Characteristics): The inherent qualities of letters.
وَبَعْدُ إِنَّ هَذِهِ مُقَدِّمَهْ
And thereafter: This is an introduction
فِيمَا عَلَى قَارِئِهِ أَنْ يَعْلَمَهْ
regarding what its reciter must know.
إِذْ وَاجِبٌ عَلَيْهِمُ مُحَتَّمُ
For it is an absolute obligation upon them
قَبْلَ الشُّرُوعِ أَوَّلاً أَنْ يَعْلَمُوا
before beginning (to recite), to first know:
مَخَارِجَ الْحُرُوفِ وَالصِّفَاتِ
The articulation points of the letters and the characteristics
لِيَلْفِظُوا بِأَفْصَحِ اللُّغَاتِ
so that they may pronounce with the most eloquent of languages.

الْوُجُوب (The Obligation of Knowledge) - Part 2

تَجْوِيد (Tajweed)

Mastering the rules of recitation, giving every letter its due right and deserved characteristic.

مَوَاقِف (Stopping Places)

Knowing exactly where to pause to preserve the divine meaning.

رَسْم الْمُصْحَف (Script of the Mushaf)

Understanding the Uthmani script, specifically cut/joined words and open/closed تَاء (Taa).

مُحَرِّرِي التَّجْوِيدِ وَالْمَوَاقِفِ
Perfecting the Tajweed and the stopping places
وَمَا الَّذِي رُسِمَ فِي الْمَصَاحِفِ
and what was drawn (written) in the Mushafs:
مِنْ كُلِّ مَقْطُوعٍ وَمَوْصُولٍ بِهَا
Of every cut and joined (word) within them
وَتَاءِ أُنْثَى لَمْ تَكُنْ تُكْتَبْ بِهَا
and the feminine Taa that was not written with it (as closed).

مَخَارِج الْحُرُوف (Articulation Points of the Letters)

  • اللِّسَان (The Tongue) - 10 Points
  • الْحَلْق (The Throat) - 3 Points
  • الشَّفَتَان (The Lips) - 2 Points
  • الْجَوْف (The Empty Space) - 1 Point
  • الْخَيْشُوم (The Nasal Cavity) - 1 Point
مَخَارِجُ الحُرُوفِ سَبْعَةَ عَشَرْ
The articulation points of the letters are seventeen
عَلَى الَّذِي يَخْتَارُهُ مَنِ اخْتَبَرْ
according to the opinion chosen by the one who tested (and investigated) it.

الْجَوْف (The Empty Space) & الْحَلْق (The Throat)

الْجَوْف (The Empty Space)

The empty space in the mouth and throat. It is the exit for the three letters of مَدّ (Elongation): Alif (ا), Waw (و), and Yaa (ي) when سَاكِنَة (Vowelless).

الْحَلْق (The Throat)

  • أَقْصَى الْحَلْق (Deepest): Hamzah (ء) and Haa (هـ)
  • وَسَط الْحَلْق (Middle): 'Ayn (ع) and Haa (ح)
فَأَلِفُ الْجَوْفِ وَأُخْتَاهَا وَهِي
So for the empty space is Alif and its two sisters, and they
حُرُوفُ مَدٍّ لِلْهَوَاءِ تَنْتَهِي
are letters of elongation ending with the air.
ثُمَّ لأَقْصَى الْحَلْقِ هَمْزٌ هَاءُ
Then for the deepest part of the throat is Hamzah and Haa
ثُمَّ لِوَسْطِهِ فَعَيْنٌ حَاءُ
Then for its middle is 'Ayn and Haa.

Upper الْحَلْق & أَقْصَى اللِّسَان (Deepest Tongue)

أَدْنَى الْحَلْق (Closest Throat)

Ghayn (غ) and Khaa (خ) originate from the topmost part of the throat, closest to the mouth.

أَقْصَى اللِّسَان (Deepest Tongue)

Moving from the throat into the mouth, the very back of the tongue produces two letters by striking the upper palate.

  • Qaaf (ق): Produced from the deepest part, striking the soft palate.
  • Kaaf (ك): Produced slightly forward from the Qaaf (slightly lower).
أَدْنَاهُ غَيْنٌ خَاؤُهَا وَالْقَافُ
Its closest part is Ghayn and Khaa, and the Qaaf
أَقْصَى اللِّسَانِ فَوْقُ ثُمَّ الْكَافُ
is from the deepest part of the tongue, above. Then the Kaaf...

وَسَط اللِّسَان (Middle) & حَافَّة اللِّسَان (Edges)

وَسَط اللِّسَان (Middle)

Jeem (ج), Sheen (ش), Yaa (ي)
Produced when the middle of the tongue rises to the hard palate.

حَافَّة اللِّسَان (Sides)

Dhaad (ض)
Produced from the side of the tongue touching the upper molars (left, right, or both).

Edge to Tip

Laam (ل)
Produced from the edges of the tongue up to its tip, touching the upper gums.

أَسْفَلُ وَالْوَسْطُ فَجِيمُ الشِّينُ يَا
...is lower. And the middle (of the tongue) is Jeem, Sheen, and Yaa
وَالضَّادُ مِنْ حَافَتِهِ إِذْ وَلِيَا
And the Dhaad is from its edge when it lies against...
الاَضْرَاسَ مِنْ أَيْسَرَ أَوْ يُمْنَاهَا
The molars, from its left or right side
وَاللاَّمُ أَدْنَاهَا لِمُنْتَهَاهَا
And the Laam is from its nearest edge to its end (tip).

طَرَف اللِّسَان (Tip of the Tongue) - Part 1

Letter Arabic Exact مَخْرَج (Articulation Point)
Noon ن From the tip of the tongue and the gums of the upper two front teeth, slightly below the Laam.
Raa ر From the tip of the tongue and the upper gums, but the back (top) of the tip curves closer to the palate.
وَالنُّونُ مِنْ طَرَفِهِ تَحْتُ اجْعَلُوا
And make the Noon from its tip, slightly below (the Laam)
وَالرَّا يُدَانِيهِ لِظَهْرٍ أَدْخَلُوا
And the Raa is close to it, but utilizing the back (top) of the tongue more.

طَرَف اللِّسَان (Tip of the Tongue) - Part 2

Category Letters Interaction Point
النَّطْعِيَّة (Palatal) ط ، د ، ت Tip of the tongue touching the roots of the upper two front teeth.
الأَسَلِيَّة (Tip) ص ، ز ، س Tip of the tongue slightly behind the lower incisors.
اللِّثَوِيَّة (Gum) ظ ، ذ ، ث Tip of the tongue touching the edges of the upper two front teeth.
وَالطَّاءُ وَالدَّالُ وَتَا مِنْهُ وَمِنْ
And Taa, Daal, and Taa are from it (the tip) and from
عُلْيَا الثَّنَايَا وَالصَّفِيرُ مُسْتَكِنْ
the upper incisors. And the whistling letters rest...
مِنْهُ وَمِنْ فَوْقِ الثَّنَايَا السُّفْلَى
From it (the tip) and just above the lower incisors
وَالظَّاءُ وَالذَّالُ وَثَا لِلْعُلْيَا
And the Dhaa, Thaal, and Thaa are for the (edges of the) upper (incisors).

الشَّفَتَان (The Lips) & الْخَيْشُوم (The Nasal Cavity)

الشَّفَتَان (The Lips)

Faa (ف): Inside of the lower lip against the edges of the upper front teeth.

Waw (و), Baa (ب), Meem (م): Produced by both lips together.

الْخَيْشُوم (The Nasal Cavity)

The empty space in the nose connecting to the back of the throat.

This is the exit point solely for the غُنَّة (Nasalization), an innate characteristic of the letters Noon (ن) and Meem (م).

مِنْ طَرَفَيْهِمَا وَمِنْ بَطْنِ الشَّفَهْ
From their edges (tongue tip & upper teeth). And from the belly of the (lower) lip
فَالْفَا مَعَ اطْرافِ الثَّنَايَا المُشْرِفَهْ
is the Faa, with the edges of the upper incisors.
لِلشَّفَتَيْنِ الْوَاوُ بَاءٌ مِيمُ
For the two lips are the Waw, Baa, and Meem
وَغُنَّةٌ مَخْرَجُهَا الخَيْشُومُ
And the Ghunnah, its articulation point is the nasal cavity.

Characteristic Meaning Opposite Meaning
هَمْس (Whisper) Breath flows جَهْر (Audibility) Breath stopped
شِدَّة (Stiffness) Sound stopped رَخَاوَة (Softness) Sound flows
اِسْتِعْلَاء (Elevation) Heavy اِسْتِفَال (Lowering) Light
إِطْبَاق (Adhesion) Enclosed اِنْفِتَاح (Openness) Separated
صِفَاتُهَا جَهْرٌ وَرِخْوٌ مُسْتَفِلْ
Its characteristics are Jahr, Rakhw, Mustafil
مُنْفَتِحٌ مُصْمَتَةٌ وَالضِّدَّ قُلْ
Munfatih, Musmatah - and say the opposites.
مَهْمُوسُهَا (فَحَثَّهُ شَخْصٌ سَكَتْ)
Its whispered (Hams) are: (fa hath-thahu shakhsun sakat)
شَدِيدُهَا لَفْظُ (أَجِدْ قَطٍ بَكَتْ)
Its stiff (Shiddah) are the wording: (ajid qatin bakat).

Elevation, Adhesion & Fluency

تَوَسُّط (Moderation)

Between stiffness and softness is moderation:
لِنْ عُمَرْ

اِسْتِعْلَاء (Elevation)

The 7 heavy letters where the tongue rises:
خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ

إِطْبَاق (Adhesion)

The 4 extremely heavy, adhered letters:
ص ، ض ، ط ، ظ

The إِذْلَاق (Fluency) letters are gathered in: فِرَّ مِنْ لُبِّ

وَبَيْنَ رِخْوٍ وَالشَّدِيدِ (لِنْ عُمَرْ)
And between the soft and the stiff is: (lin 'umar)
وَسَبْعُ عُلْوٍ (خُصَّ ضَغْطٍ قِظْ) حَصَرْ
And the seven of elevation are restricted in: (khussa daghtin qidh).
وَصَادُ ضَادٌ طَاءُ ظَاءٌ مُطْبَقَهْ
And Saad, Dhaad, Taa, and Dhaa are the adhered (Mutbaqah) letters
وَفِرَّ مِنْ لُبِّ الحُرُوفُ المُذْلَقَهْ
And (firra min lubbi) are the fluent (Mudlaqah) letters.

صِفَات (Characteristics) without Opposites

Characteristic Meaning Letters
صَفِير (Whistling) A sound resembling a bird's whistle. ص ، ز ، س
قَلْقَلَة (Echo) A strong reverberation when the letter is سَاكِنَة. قُطْبُ جَدٍّ
لِين (Softness) Ease of pronunciation without force. Waw (و) & Yaa (ي) سَاكِنَة preceded by فَتْحَة
اِنْحِرَاف & تَكْرِير Deviation (Laam/Raa) and Repetition (Raa only). ل ، ر
صَفِيرُهَا صَادٌ وَزَايٌ سِينُ
Its whistling (Safeer) is Saad, Zaay, and Seen
قَلْقَلَةٌ قُطْبُ جَدٍّ وَاللِّينُ
Qalqalah is (qutbu jaddin). And the Leen...
وَاوٌ وَيَاءٌ سَكَنَا وَانْفَتَحَا
Are Waw and Yaa when they are Saakin and preceded by Fathah
قَبْلَهُمَا وَالاِنْحِرَافُ صُحِّحَا
And Deviation (Inhiraf) is established...
فِي اللاَّمِ وَالرَّا وَبِتَكْرِيرٍ جُعِلْ
In the Laam and the Raa, and (the Raa) is made with Repetition (Takreer)
وَلِلتَّفَشِّي الشِّينُ ضَادًا اسْتُطِلْ
And for Diffusion (Tafash-shee) is the Sheen, and the Dhaad is Elongated (Istitaalah).

The Necessity of تَجْوِيد (Tajweed)

Applying Tajweed is an absolute obligation; whoever does not correct the Quran is a sinner.

Because with it (Tajweed) Allah revealed it, and thus from Him it reached us.

وَالأَخْذُ بِالتَّجْوِيدِ حَتْمٌ لازِمُ
And applying Tajweed is an absolute obligation
مَنْ لَمْ يُجَوِّدِ الْقُرَآنَ آثِمُ
Whoever does not correct the Quran is a sinner.
لأَنَّهُ بِهِ الإِلَهُ أَنْزَلاَ
Because with it, the God revealed it
وَهَكَذَا مِنْهُ إِلَيْنَا وَصَلاَ
and exactly like that, it reached us from Him.
وَهُوَ أَيْضًا حِلْيَةُ التِّلاَوَةِ
And it is also the adornment of recitation
وَزِينَةُ الأَدَاءِ وَالْقِرَاءَةِ
and the embellishment of performance and reading.

The Essence of Practice

حَقّ (Inherent Right)

Tajweed involves giving every letter its inherent right (حَقّ) and its deserved characteristic (مُسْتَحَقّ) based on context.

Gentleness

Pronouncing the letters with gentleness and ease, avoiding harshness, strain, or forced artificiality in speech.

وَهُوَ إِعْطَاءُ الْحُرُوفِ حَقَّهَا
And it is giving the letters their due right
مِنْ صِفَةٍ لَهَا وَمُسْتَحَقَّهَا
of every characteristic they possess, and their deserved right.
وَرَدُّ كُلِّ وَاحِدٍ لأَصْلِهِ
And returning each one to its origin
وَاللَّفْظُ فِي نَظِيرِهِ كَمِثْلِهِ
and pronouncing its counterpart similarly.
مُكَمَّلاً مِنْ غَيْرِ مَا تَكَلُّفِ
Completed without any artificiality
بِاللُّطْفِ فِي النُّطْقِ بِلاَ تَعَسُّفِ
with gentleness in pronunciation without harshness.
وَلَيْسَ بَيْنَهُ وَبَيْنَ تَرْكِهِ
And there is nothing between it and leaving it
إِلاَّ رِيَاضَةُ امْرِئٍ بِفَكِّهِ
except a person's exercise with their jaw.

تَرْقِيق (Thinning)

اِسْتِفَال (Lowering)

All lowered letters must be pronounced thinly (تَرْقِيق). Do not mix them with heavy letters (تَفْخِيم).

The Alif

Take immense care not to make the Alif heavy when it follows a light letter. It follows the letter before it.

فَرَقِّقَنْ مُسْتَفِلاً مِنْ أَحْرُفِ
So always thin (Tarqeeq) the lowered (Mustafil) letters
وَحَاذِرَنْ تَفْخِيمَ لَفْظِ الأَلِفِ
And beware of making the pronunciation of Alif heavy.
وَهَمْزَ اَلْحَمْدِ أَعُوذُ إِهْدِنَا
And (thin) the Hamzah of 'Al-Hamd', 'A'oodhu', 'Ihdina', 'Allah'
اللَّهِ ثُمَّ لاَمَ لِلَّهِ لَنَا
then the Laam of 'Lillahi', 'Lana'.

And the Seen of 'Mustaqeem', 'Yastoo', 'Yasqoo'.

Rules of the Letter Raa (أحكام الراء)

تَرْقِيق (Light)

The Raa is made light when it has a كَسْرَة (Kasrah).

Also light if سَاكِنَة, preceded by Kasrah, and not followed by an elevated letter.

تَكْرِير (Repetition)

Hide (suppress) the repetitive trilling nature of the Raa, especially when it carries a شَدَّة (Shaddah).

وَرَقِّقِ الرَّاءَ إِذَا مَا كُسِرَتْ
And make the Raa light when it is vowelled with a Kasrah
كَذَاكَ بَعْدَ الْكَسْرِ حَيْثُ سَكَنَتْ
Likewise, after a Kasrah when it is Saakin.
إِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ مِنْ قَبْلِ حَرْفِ اسْتِعْلاَ
Provided it is not before a letter of elevation
أَوْ كَانَتِ الْكَسْرَةُ لَيْسَتْ أَصْلاَ
or if the Kasrah is not original.
وَالْخُلْفُ فِي فِرْقٍ لِكَسْرٍ يُوجَدُ
And there is difference of opinion in 'Firq' because of the Kasrah present
وَأَخْفِ تَكْرِيرًا إِذَا تُشَدَّدُ
And hide the repetition when it is doubled (Shaddah).

Rules of Laam & Preserving إِطْبَاق (Adhesion)

Concept Rule Application
Laam in Allah's Name Make the Laam of 'Allah' heavy (تَفْخِيم) if preceded by a فَتْحَة (Fathah) or ضَمَّة (Dammah).
Preserving إِطْبَاق The extreme heaviness of Taa (ط), Dhaad (ض), Saad (ص), and Dhaa (ظ) makes them stronger than mere elevated letters.
وَفَخِّمِ اللاَّمَ مِنِ اسْمِ اللَّهِ
And make the Laam heavy in the name of 'Allah'
عَنْ فَتْحٍ اوْ ضَمٍّ كَعَبْدُ اللَّهِ
following a Fathah or Dammah, like 'Abdu-llah'.
وَحَرْفَ الاِسْتِعْلاَءِ فَخِّمْ وَاخْصُصَا
And make the letters of elevation heavy, and specifically
الاِطْبَاقَ أَقْوَى نَحْوُ قَالَ وَالْعَصَا
make Itbaaq stronger, like in 'Qaala' and 'Al-Asaa'.
وَبَيِّنِ الإِطْبَاقَ مِنْ أَحَطتُّ مَعْ
And make the Itbaaq clear in 'Ahattu' along with
بَسَطتَّ وَالْخُلْفُ بِنَخْلُقكُّمْ وَقَعْ
'Basatta'. And a difference of opinion occurred in 'Nakhluqkum'.

إِدْغَام (Merging)

مُتَمَاثِلَيْن (Two Identical Letters)

Merging two identical letters where the first is سَاكِنَة. Mandatory to merge.

مُتَجَانِسَيْن (Two Similar Letters)

Merging two letters that share the same Makhraj but have different Sifaat (e.g., Taa into Daal).

Caution: إِظْهَار (Clarity)

Do NOT merge if the first letter is a Madd letter to preserve the elongation.

وَاحْرِصْ عَلَى السُّكُونِ فِي جَعَلْنَا
And be careful (to show) the Sukoon in 'Ja'alnaa'
أَنْعَمْتَ وَالْمَغْضُوبِ مَعْ ضَلَلْنَا
'An'amta', 'Al-Maghdoob' along with 'Dalalnaa'.
وَخَلِّصِ انْفِتَاحَ مَحْذُورًا عَسَى
And clarify the openness of 'Mahdhooran' and 'Asaa'
خَوْفَ اشْتِبَاهِهِ بِمَحْظُورًا عَصَى
fearing confusion with 'Mahdhooran' and 'Asaa' (heavy versions).
وَأَدْغِمْ كَطَاءِ مَعْ دَالٍ وَتَا
And merge like Taa with Daal and Taa
وَقُلْ نَعَمْ وَسَبِّحْهُ لاَ تُزِغْ قُلُوبَ
And (make clear) 'Qul Na'am' and 'Sabbih-hu' and 'La Tuzigh Qulooba'.

Distinguishing الض (Dhaad) from الظ (Dhaa)

The Challenge

Because الض and الظ are both heavy and adhered (إِطْبَاق), they are easily confused. However, their articulation points are entirely different.

  • Dhaad (ض): Uses اِسْتِطَالَة (Elongation of sound across the side of the tongue).
  • Dhaa (ظ): Pronounced from the tip of the tongue and the edges of the upper teeth.

Due to the difficulty, Imam Al-Jazari lists all occurrences of الظ in the Quran.

وَالضَّادَ بِاسْتِطَالَةٍ وَمَخْرَجِ
And the Dhaad, by its elongation (Istitaalah) and articulation point
مَيِّزْ مِنَ الظَّاءِ وَكُلُّهَا تَجِي
distinguish it from the Dhaa. And all of them come (in the following contexts)...

Occurrences of الظ (Dhaa) - Part 1

Meaning Word (Arabic) Meaning Word (Arabic)
The Journey الظَّعْن The Shadow الظِّلّ
The Noon الظُّهْر The Greatness الْعُظْم
The Protection الْحِفْظ Wake up أَيْقِظْ
فِي الظَّعْنِ ظِلُّ الظُّهْرِ عُظْمِ الْحِفْظِ
In Dha'n, Dhill, Dhuhr, 'Udhm, Hifdh
أَيْقِظْ وَأَنْظُرْ عَظْمِ ظَهْرِ اللَّفْظِ
Ayqidh, Andhur, 'Adhm, Dhahr, Lafdh.
ظَاهِرْ لَظَى شُوَاظِ كَظْمٍ ظَلَمَا

أُغْلُظْ ظَلاَمِ ظُفْرٍ انْتَظِرْ ظَمَا
Ughludh, Dhalaam, Dhufr, Antadhir, Dhamaa.

Critical Separation of Letters

Meeting of Dhaad and Dhaa

When Dhaad (ض) or Dhaa (ظ) meets another heavy letter or a Taa (ت), you must clearly separate them using إِظْهَار (Clarity).

Example: أَنقَضَ ظَهْرَكَ (Anqadha Dhahrak).

Meem/Baa and Faa/Waw

When Meem (م) or Baa (ب) meets a Faa (ف) or Waw (و), ensure strict إِظْهَار (Clarity).

Because they share the same Makhraj (the lips), the tongue will naturally want to merge them.

وَالْحَظُّ لاَ الْحَضُّ عَلَى الطَّعَامِ
And Al-Hadh (fortune), not Al-Hadd (encouraging) upon feeding
وَفِي ضَنِينٍ الْخِلاَفُ سَامِي
And in 'Daneen' the disagreement is known.
وَإِنْ تَلاَقَيَا الْبَيَانُ لاَزِمُ
And if they (Dhaad and Dhaa) meet, clarity is mandatory
أَنْقَضَ ظَهْرَكَ يَعَضُّ الظَّالِمُ
'Anqadha Dhahraka', 'Ya'addud-Dhaalimu'.
وَاضْطُرَّ مَعْ وَعَظْتَ مَعْ أَفَضْتُمُ
And 'Udturra' with 'Wa'adhta' with 'Afadtumu'
وَصَفِّ هَا جِبَاهُهُمْ عَلَيْهِمُ
And make clear the Haa of 'Jibaahuhum', 'Alayhimu'.

Meem & Noon مُشَدَّدَة / Meem سَاكِنَة

غُنَّة (Nasalization)

Always hold a 2-count nasal sound for Noon and Meem when they have a شَدَّة.

إِخْفَاء شَفَوِيّ (Labial Hiding)

If Meem Saakinah meets Baa (ب), hide the Meem gently with Ghunnah.

إِظْهَار شَفَوِيّ (Labial Clarity)

If Meem Saakinah meets Waw (و) or Faa (ف), keep the Meem completely clear to avoid hiding.

وَأَظْهِرِ الْغُنَّةَ مِنْ نُونٍ وَمِنْ
And make the Ghunnah clear from Noon and from
مِيمٍ إِذَا مَا شُدِّدَا وَأَخْفِيَنْ
Meem when they are doubled. And hide...
الْمِيمَ إِنْ تَسْكُنْ بِغُنَّةٍ لَدَى
The Meem if it is Saakin with Ghunnah at a
بَاءٍ عَلَى الْمُخْتَارِ مِنْ أَهْلِ الأَدَا
Baa, according to the chosen opinion of the people of performance.
وَأَظْهِرَنْهَا عِنْدَ بَاقِي الأَحْرُفِ
And make it clear at the rest of the letters
وَاحْذَرْ لَدَى وَاوٍ وَفَا أَنْ تَخْتَفِي
And beware, at a Waw and Faa, that it hides.

Rules of Noon Saakinah & تَنْوِين (Tanween)

Rule Letters Action
إِظْهَار (Clarity) ء هـ ع ح غ خ Pronounce the Noon clearly without extra Ghunnah.
إِدْغَام (Merging) ي ر م ل و ن Merge the Noon into the next letter.
إِقْلَاب (Conversion) ب Turn the Noon into a hidden Meem with Ghunnah.
إِخْفَاء (Hiding) Remaining 15 Letters Hide the Noon near the next letter, keeping Ghunnah.
وَحُكْمُ تَنْوِينٍ وَنُونٍ يُلْفَى
And the rule of Tanween and Noon is found to be:
إِظْهَارٌ ادْغَامٌ وَقَلْبٌ إِخْفَا
Clarity (Izhaar), Merging (Idghaam), Conversion (Qalb), and Hiding (Ikhfaa).
فَعِنْدَ حَرْفِ الْحَلْقِ أَظْهِرْ وَادْغِمْ
So at a throat letter, make it clear. And merge
فِي اللامِ وَالرَّا لاَ بِغُنَّةٍ لَزِمْ
in the Laam and Raa, strictly without Ghunnah.
وَأَدْغِمَنْ بِغُنَّةٍ فِي يُومِنُ
And merge with Ghunnah in 'yoominu' (ي ن م و)
إِلاَّ بِكِلْمَةٍ كَدُنْيَا عَنْوَنُوا
unless it is in one word like 'Dunya' they titled.
وَالْقَلْبُ عِنْدَ الْبَا بِغُنَّةٍ كَذَا
And Conversion is at the Baa with Ghunnah. Likewise
لاِخْفَا لَدَى بَاقِي الحُرُوفِ أُخِذَا
Hiding at the rest of the letters is taken.

Rules of مَدّ (Lengthening)

Category Condition Duration & Example
وَاجِب (Mandatory) Madd letter + Hamzah in the same word. 4 or 5 counts. (e.g., السَّمَاء).
جَائِز (Permissible) Madd letter + Hamzah in separate words. 2, 4, or 5 counts. (e.g., بِمَا أُنزِلَ).
لَازِم (Compulsory) Madd letter + Original Sukoon/Shaddah. Strictly 6 counts. (e.g., الضَّالِّينَ).
وَالْمَدُّ لاَزِمٌ وَوَاجِبٌ أَتَى
And Madd comes as Compulsory (Laazim), Mandatory (Waajib)
وَجَائِزٌ وَهْوَ وَقَصْرٌ ثَبَتَا
and Permissible (Jaa'iz), and it and shortness are established.
فَلاَزِمٌ إِنْ جَاءَ بَعْدَ حَرْفِ مَدْ
So it is Compulsory if after a Madd letter comes
سَاكِنُ حَالَيْنِ وَبِالطُّولِ يُمَدْ
a Saakin in both states (stopping/continuing), and it is lengthened fully.
وَوَاجِبٌ إِنْ جَاءَ قَبْلَ هَمْزَةِ
And Mandatory if it comes before a Hamzah
مُتَّصِلاً إِنْ جُمِعَا بِكِلْمَةِ
connected, if they are gathered in one word.
وَجَائِزٌ إِذَا أَتَى مُنْفَصِلاَ
And Permissible if it comes separated
أَوْ عَرَضَ السُّكُونُ وَقْفًا مُسْجَلاَ
or if the Sukoon is temporary due to stopping absolutely.

مَعْرِفَة الْوَقْف (Knowing the Stops) - Part 1

تَامّ (Complete)

Meaning and grammar are completely detached from what follows. (Stop & Start after).

كَاف (Sufficient)

Meaning is connected, but grammar is complete. (Stop & Start after).

حَسَن (Good)

Meaning and grammar are strongly connected. (You may stop, but must repeat to start).

وَبَعْدَ تَجْوِيدِكَ لِلْحُرُوفِ
And after your Tajweed of the letters
لاَبُدَّ مِنْ مَعْرِفَةِ الْوُقُوفِ
it is absolutely necessary to know the stops.
وَالاِبْتِدَاءِ وَهْيَ تُقْسَمُ إِذَنْ
And the starts. And they are divided then
ثَلاَثَةً تَامٌ وَكَافٍ وَحَسَنْ
into three: Complete (Taamm), Sufficient (Kaaf), and Good (Hasan).
وَهْيَ لِمَا تَمَّ فَإِنْ لَمْ يُوجَدِ
And they are for what is complete. So if there is no connection found
تَعَلُّقٌ أَوْ كَانَ مَعْنًى فَابْتَدِي
or only in meaning, then initiate.

مَعْرِفَة الْوَقْف (Knowing the Stops) - Part 2

حَسَن (Good) and Verses

Normally, if a sentence is grammatically connected, you should not start from there. EXCEPT at the end of a verse (Ayah). It is Sunnah and permissible to stop and start.

قَبِيح (Repulsive)

Stopping where it corrupts the meaning (e.g., stopping at "do not pray..." without finishing the condition). If forced to stop by breath, you must repeat to correct the meaning.

فَالتَّامُ فَالْكَافِي وَلَفْظًا فَامْنَعَنْ
So the Taamm, then the Kaaf. And if grammatically connected, then prevent (initiating)
إِلاَّ رُؤُوسَ الآيِ جَوِّزْ فَالْحَسَنْ
except at the heads of verses, permit it, so it is Hasan.
وَغَيْرُ مَا تَمَّ قَبِيحٌ وَلَهُ
And other than what is complete is Repulsive (Qabeeh), and for it
يُوقَفُ مُضْطَرًّا وَيُبْدَا قَبْلَهُ
one stops out of necessity, and initiates before it.

Script: الْمَقْطُوع (Cut) & الْمَوْصُول (Joined)

الْمَقْطُوع (Cut)

Words that are written separately in the Uthmani script (e.g., أَنْ لَا vs أَلَّا). If forced by breath, a reciter may stop on the first word.

الْمَوْصُول (Joined)

Words that are joined together as one word in the script (e.g., مِمَّا). It is forbidden to stop in the middle of a joined word.

وَاعْرِفْ لِمَقْطُوعٍ وَمَوْصُولٍ وَتَا
And know the cut and joined words, and the Taa
فِي مُصْحَفِ الإِمَامِ فِيمَا قَدْ أَتَى
in the Mushaf of the Imam (Uthman) regarding what has come.
فَاقْطَعْ بِعَشْرِ كَلِمَاتٍ أَنْ لاَ
So cut 'An Laa' in ten words
مَعْ مَلْجَإٍ وَلاَ إِلَهَ إِلاَّ
with 'Malja'in', and 'La Ilaha Illa'...

Feminine تَاء (Taa) Written Open (ت)

Word Context in the Quran (Written as ت instead of ة)
رَحْمَت (Rahmat) Al-Baqarah, Al-A'raf, Room, Luqman, Fatir, Maryam (e.g., ذِكْرُ رَحْمَتِ رَبِّكَ)
نِعْمَت (Ni'mat) Al-Baqarah, Aal 'Imran, Al-Ma'idah, Ibrahim, An-Nahl, Fatir

If forced to stop, you must stop with a "T" sound, not an "H" sound, following the script.

وَرَحْمَتُ الزُّخْرُفِ بِالتَّا زَبَرَهْ
And 'Rahmat' in Az-Zukhruf is written with Taa
الاَعْرَافِ رُومٍ هُودٍ كَافِ الْبَقَرَهْ
(And in) Al-A'raf, Room, Hood, Kaaf (Maryam), Al-Baqarah.
نِعْمَتُهَا ثَلاَثُ نَحْلٍ إِبْرَهَمْ
'Ni'mat' in it (Al-Baqarah), three in An-Nahl, Ibrahim
مَعَا أَخِيرَاتُ عُقُودُ الثَّانِ هَمْ
together, the last ones of Al-Uqood (Al-Ma'idah), the second of 'Ham' (Ghafir).

Starting with هَمْزَةُ الْوَصْل (Connecting Hamzah)

ضَمَّة (Dammah)

Start with a Dammah if the third letter of the verb has an original Dammah (e.g., ٱدْعُ).

كَسْرَة (Kasrah)

Start with a Kasrah if the third letter has a فَتْحَة (Fathah) or Kasrah (e.g., ٱضْرِب).

Specific Nouns

Always Kasrah on specific nouns: ابْن، امْرِئ، اثْنَيْن. And Fathah for the definite article (ال).

وَابْدَأْ بِهَمْزِ الْوَصْلِ مِنْ فِعْلٍ بِضَمْ
And begin with Hamzat Al-Wasl of a verb with a Dammah
إِنْ كَانَ ثَالِثٌ مِنَ الْفِعْلِ يُضَمْ
if the third letter of the verb has a Dammah.
وَكْسِرْهُ حَالَ الْكَسْرِ وَالْفَتْحِ وَفِي
And break it (Kasrah) in the case of Kasrah and Fathah. And in
الاَسْمَاءِ غَيْرَ اللاَّمِ كَسْرَهَا وَفِي
the nouns other than the 'Laam' (definite article), its breaking is fulfilled. And in:
ابْنٍ مَعَ ابْنَةِ امْرِئٍ وَاثْنَيْنِ
Ibn with Ibnat, Imri'in, and Ithnayn
وَامْرَأَةٍ وَاسْمٍ مَعَ اثْنَتَيْنِ
And Imra'atin, and Ism, with Ithnatayn.

Khatimat Al-Mandhoomah (Conclusion)

This concludes the structured foundation of تَجْوِيد (Tajweed) as laid out by Imam Al-Jazari. Mastery requires dedication, a sincere heart, and the continuous guidance of a qualified teacher.

وَحَاذِرِ الْوَقْفَ بِكُلِّ الحَرَكَهْ
And beware of stopping with the full vowel
إِلاَّ إِذَا رُمْتَ فَبَعْضُ حَرَكَهْ
except if you seek Roum (partial vowel), then a portion of the vowel.
إِلاَّ بِفَتْحٍ أَوْ بِنَصْبٍ وَأَشِمْ
Except with Fathah or Nasb. And do Ishmaam
إِشَارَةً بِالضَّمِّ فِي رَفْعٍ وَضَمْ
an indication by the lips of Dammah in Raf' (Nominative) and Dammah.
وَقَدْ تَقَضَّى نَظْمِيَ الْمُقَدِّمَهْ
And my poem, The Introduction, has concluded
مِنِّي لِقَارِئِ الْقُرْآنِ تَقْدِمَهْ
an offering from me to the reciter of the Quran.
وَالْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ لَهَا خِتَامُ
And praise be to Allah is its conclusion
ثُمَّ الصَّلاَةُ بَعْدُ وَالسَّلاَمُ
Then blessings thereafter, and peace.
عَلَى النَّبِيِّ الْمُصْطَفَى وَآلِهِ
Upon the chosen Prophet and his family
وَصَحْبِهِ وَتَابِعِي مِنْوَالِهِ
and his companions and the followers of his way.
